3 Case Brief & Certified Ratio

Facts & Procedural Genesis

Following security advisories and constitutional alterations regarding Article 370 in early August 2019, the State imposed widespread communication blackouts, cutting mobile, broadband, and landline services across Jammu & Kashmir, paired with district-wide Section 144 CrPC movement restrictions.

Core Issues Examined by the Court

1. Whether freedom of speech and expression and the right to carry on trade/business over the internet are fundamental rights under Article 19.
2. Whether the indefinite suspension of telecom services without publishing executive orders violates the doctrine of proportionality.
3. Whether Section 144 of the CrPC can be used to prevent legitimate democratic assembly and expression.

Operative Held & Judicial Reasoning

Held: Freedom of speech and trade over the internet is constitutionally protected under Articles 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g). Indefinite internet suspensions are impermissible. All restriction orders must be published and periodically reviewed every 7 working days by a Review Committee.
Reasoning: The Court applied the four-pronged test of proportionality: legitimate goal, rational nexus, least intrusive means, and absence of disproportionate impact.

4 The 8-Episode Sequential Arc

EPISODE 01 · THE PREMISE: The Midnight Communications Blackout

Story Narrative: On August 4, 2019, all mobile networks, broadband connections, and landlines were terminated across Jammu & Kashmir without advance warning or published executive decrees.
Student Layer (Ratio & Obiter): Ratio: Internet medium is protected under Article 19(1)(a). Obiter: The digital sphere is indispensable to democratic participation. Exam Angle: Proportionality review of telecom shutdowns for CLAT-PG.
Advocate Layer: TRIAL PROPOSITION: Indefinite suspension of internet connectivity without temporal boundaries is ultra vires Article 19 and the Telecom Suspension Rules, 2017. [¶24, ¶88]

EPISODE 02 · DRAMATIS PERSONAE: The Editor, the Parliamentarian, and the State

Story Narrative: Anuradha Bhasin and Ghulam Nabi Azad moved the Supreme Court under Article 32, alleging total suppression of press freedom and basic liberties.
Advocate Layer: TRIAL PROPOSITION: Blanket communication curbs that completely paralyze newspaper publication violate the freedom of the press under Article 19(1)(a). [¶31]

EPISODE 03 · THE INVESTIGATION: The Veil of Unpublished Orders

Story Narrative: When asked to produce the restriction orders, the State initially claimed executive privilege, arguing national security shielded the orders from public disclosure.
Advocate Layer: TRIAL PROPOSITION: The State must publish all executive orders restricting fundamental liberties so affected citizens can seek judicial review. [¶18, ¶26]

EPISODE 04 · THE CONTROVERSY: Section 144 as a Weapon of Convenience

Story Narrative: District Magistrates routinely issued repetitive Section 144 CrPC curfew orders covering entire regions for months without citing specific imminent danger.
Advocate Layer: TRIAL PROPOSITION: Section 144 CrPC cannot be used as a routine administrative tool to suppress legitimate expression or dissent. [¶42, ¶99]

EPISODE 05 · COURTROOM ARGUMENTS: National Security vs. Democratic Rights

Story Narrative: The Solicitor General argued that cross-border terrorism necessitated preemptive curbs; Senior Counsel argued that security cannot extinguish constitutional existence.
Advocate Layer: TRIAL PROPOSITION: National security claims do not grant the State total immunity from constitutional proportionality scrutiny. [¶64, ¶71]

EPISODE 06 · JUDICIAL DELIBERATION: The Four-Pronged Proportionality Standard

Story Narrative: The Bench examined international human rights standards and domestic constitutional tests, applying the Modern Dental College and Puttaswamy proportionality doctrines.
Advocate Layer: TRIAL PROPOSITION: State restrictions on fundamental rights must adopt the least restrictive measure available to achieve legitimate aims. [¶102, ¶108]

EPISODE 07 · THE RATIO & OPERATIVE HOLDING: The Mandate of Periodic Review

Story Narrative: The Supreme Court directed immediate review of all suspension orders, ordered restoration of essential websites, and mandated 7-day review cycles.
Advocate Layer: TRIAL PROPOSITION: An order suspending telecom services under the 2017 Rules cannot be permanent and must be reviewed every 7 working days. [¶124, ¶130]

EPISODE 08 · PRECEDENTIAL LEGACY: The Global Digital Charter

Story Narrative: Anuradha Bhasin became India's foundational digital rights jurisprudence, cited across common law jurisdictions balancing cybersecurity and civil liberty.
Advocate Layer: TRIAL PROPOSITION: The right to internet access as a conduit for trade, profession, and speech is non-negotiable in 21st-century jurisprudence. [¶142]

Part 1 · Statutory Text As Reproduced In The Judgment

1. Temporary Suspension of Telecom Services Rules, 2017 — Rule 2(2):
"An order issued by the competent authority... shall not be valid for more than seven working days unless confirmed by the Review Committee." (Pinpoint: SCR p. 45, Para 38)

2. Constitution of India — Article 19(1)(a) & 19(1)(g):
"All citizens shall have the right to freedom of speech and expression; and to practise any profession, or to carry on any occupation, trade or business."

3. Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 — Section 144:
"Power to issue order in urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger."

Part 2 · The Court's Enumerated Holdings

Holding 1: Freedom of speech and expression and the freedom to practice any trade or business through the medium of the internet is constitutionally protected under Article 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(g). [¶111, SCC p. 680]
Holding 2: An order suspending internet services indefinitely is impermissible under the Temporary Suspension of Telecom Services Rules, 2017. [¶124, SCC p. 688]
Holding 3: Section 144 CrPC cannot be used as a tool to prevent the legitimate expression of democratic opinions. [¶99, SCC p. 675]
Holding 4: The State is obligated to publish all suspension and restriction orders to ensure transparent judicial review. [¶18, SCC p. 650]

6 Student Mode Revision Flashcards

Flashcard #1 · Question: Is internet access constitutionally protected under Indian fundamental rights?
Verified Judicial Answer: Yes. While not a standalone right, the Supreme Court held that freedom of speech (Art. 19(1)(a)) and the right to carry on trade/profession (Art. 19(1)(g)) using the internet as a medium are fundamental rights. [Pinpoint: (2020) 3 SCC 637, ¶111]

Flashcard #2 · Question: Can the Government impose indefinite internet shutdowns in India?
Verified Judicial Answer: No. Indefinite shutdowns are unconstitutional. All suspension orders must be temporary, reasoned, published, and reviewed every 7 working days by a statutory Review Committee. [Pinpoint: Rule 2(2), Telecom Rules 2017; ¶124]

Flashcard #3 · Question: What test must the State satisfy before restricting telecommunications or applying Section 144 CrPC?
Verified Judicial Answer: The State must satisfy the doctrine of proportionality: (1) Legitimate state goal, (2) Rational nexus, (3) Least restrictive measure available, and (4) Proportional balance of impact. [Pinpoint: ¶102]
